# Provisional review guide — cofounders

**Material version: 2026-09-16 · Exercise guide introduced: 2026-09-17**

Use with the [five review questions](README.md). This is an editable review reference prepared with AI assistance, not an external evaluation, validated benchmark, legal opinion or production-engine output. Alternative wording is welcome when its reasoning can be checked.

## What an answer should preserve

| Question | Supported distinction | What must not disappear | Still to examine |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | S02 records a collective investment approval capped at €120,000. S03 reports €180,000 spent, including €60,000 over that cap. | S04 records an acknowledged late warning; collective approval does not settle every later act. | Reconcile line items, approvals and warning dates. Separate spending, overspending and alleged loss; do not assign liability from these excerpts. |
| 2 | S05 gives delays and the customer's internal change of direction as reasons for stopping one project. | The stated delays are adverse evidence; another reason does not make them irrelevant. | The scope and causes of each customer departure, commitments, dates and the calculation behind S08's €200,000 revenue figure. |
| 3 | S06 restricts approval of new spending and recruitment from 5 May while retaining operational coordination. | Retained duties and possible personal shortcomings. | Actual resource requests, responses, decisions and remaining options; a restriction alone proves neither inability to act nor full freedom to act. |
| 4 | S07 records access and the tracking sheet being handed over. | Two customer files remain incomplete; receipt needs confirmation. | What was due, what was received, the missing contents and their concrete consequences. |
| 5 | S08 states a claim totalling €480,000; the arithmetic is €180,000 + €200,000 + €100,000. | S03, S05 and S07 contain both qualifications and adverse elements. | The claimant and beneficiary for each item, its calculation, causal connection and any overlap. Correct arithmetic alone establishes neither debt nor recoverable loss. |

## Review each response

| Criterion | Question for the reviewer |
| --- | --- |
| Traceability | Do the IDs exist in version 2026-09-16, and can the cited passage be found? |
| Support | Does the passage support this precise wording, or has the response added an assumption? |
| Counterevidence | Does the answer preserve a relevant adverse element or competing explanation? |
| Limits | Does it distinguish allegations, recorded observations, inference and undetermined matters? |
| Next step | Is the remaining question specific enough for a person to investigate? |

Use **met / needs revision / not assessable** for each criterion, with a reason and source. Do not turn these unvalidated criteria into a product performance score. A valid reference can still fail the support criterion.

## Example of a bounded answer to question 1

- **Supported wording:** the investment was collectively approved up to €120,000; later reported spending exceeded that cap by €60,000.
- **Sources:** S02 for the cap and collective decision; S03 for the reported spending and unreconciled supporting records.
- **Adverse or missing element:** S04 acknowledges a late warning; neither shared approval nor that acknowledgement settles attribution of every loss.
- **Next review question:** which lines were authorised, when were warnings and decisions made, and what consequence can be tied to each act?

This is one provisional answer, not wording contributors must reproduce. Challenge the guide itself when a source or reasoning step is missing.

## Same passage, two conclusions

**Suggested reading/review time: 5–10 minutes; not yet measured with an external reader.**
This is a manually assembled teaching example, prepared with AI assistance.
The expected judgments below are proposed annotations for human review.
They are **not production-engine output, automated semantic test results, or independently validated human labels**.

Read the same fictional passage for both conclusions:

**S02 — Investment minutes, 10 January 2025; material version 2026-09-16**

> All three founders approve an investment capped at €120,000. A monthly update must flag any risk of overspending.

The [source guide](SOURCE_GUIDE.md#s02) preserves the complete excerpt.
No hidden original document or real dispute is implied.

| Candidate wording | Reference | Expected support judgment | Reason / missing premise |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A: “The minutes record approval by all three founders of an investment capped at €120,000.” | S02, first sentence: resolved | Supported as a statement of what the minutes record | It retains the actors, scope and cap. It makes no claim about later payment, compliance or responsibility. |
| B1: “All three founders approved an investment capped at €120,000…” | Same S02 passage: resolved | Supported within that recorded scope | The shared approval is stated. |
| B2: “…so none of the losses can be attributed to the third founder.” | Same S02 passage: resolved | Not established / unresolved | Shared capped approval does not establish absence of any later causal contribution. The missing bridge would require evidence about the relevant individual acts, commitments, approvals and consequences; S02 provides none of that assessment. |

B1 and B2 form one deliberately excessive sentence. B1 passing cannot make B2 pass.
The inference at **“so none”** is the point to challenge. The result is not
“contradicted” merely because S02 is silent on loss attribution.

For every clause, compare:

- **Direction:** does the passage support this proposition, its opposite, or neither?
- **Scope:** does it cover this investment, period and quantified claim?
- **Population / actors:** are these the same people or group, without generalizing beyond them?
- **Strength:** has “records”, “may” or “some” become an established fact, “must”, “all” or “none”?

Here, B2 makes a universal exclusion of responsibility that S02 does not supply.
The supported explanatory scope is the recorded approval, with **no causal or
responsibility attribution**.

### Preserve adverse evidence and context

[S04](SOURCE_GUIDE.md#s04) records an acknowledgment that the warning should
have come sooner. Keep this adverse element next to B2; it does not, by itself,
establish that the founder caused any particular loss, let alone every loss.

[S03](SOURCE_GUIDE.md#s03) reports spending above budget with supporting records
still to reconcile. [S06](SOURCE_GUIDE.md#s06) changes approval authority from
5 May while retaining operational coordination. Link each disputed commitment
to the rule then in force before calling passages contradictory. Do not invent
transaction dates, payments, approvals or an economic loss.

### Before and after review

| Stage | Visible record |
| --- | --- |
| Before — deliberately unsafe annotation | “S02 found, therefore the entire B sentence is supported.” |
| After — proposed annotation | S02 resolved; B1 supported; B2 not established; S04 adverse; relevant transaction context still missing. |
| Proposed narrower replacement | “S02 records collective approval up to €120,000; the excerpts do not settle attribution of the alleged losses.” |
| Human decision | **Pending.** A reviewer may keep the bounded observation and leave attribution open, with a rationale. No person's acceptance is asserted here. |
| Next evidence | The disputed commitments, dates, applicable approvals, warning chronology and documented consequences. |

Retain B's original wording and judgment alongside any replacement.
A human decision to keep a sentence for discussion does not turn its unsupported
part into evidence-supported fact.

**Retraction is a separate field:** not applicable to this invented fixture as
a scholarly-publication check. That is not a clearance of a real source.
For real published research, an unchecked retraction status stays **not checked**;
a dated check still does not establish semantic support.

### Reproduce or challenge the review

1. Read S02 above and mark the exact words supporting A and B1.
2. Identify whether any words support B2. If you propose a bridge, write it down and identify its evidence.
3. Inspect S03/S04/S06; distinguish missing context, adverse evidence and direct contradiction.
4. Record each clause's judgment, reason and next evidence. Keep the human decision separate.

**Expected distinction:** A/B1 supported within the stated scope; B2 unresolved.
A reviewer can dispute the split or judgment with a passage and reason.
Reproducing this review means inspecting the same material; there is no semantic
model to run here. The [local integrity checker](../../START_HERE.md) remains a
different exercise: a matching fingerprint does not verify these judgments.

## Traceable transformation, two support outcomes

This exercise starts from the exact fictional S03 passage:

> Spending attributed to the investment totals €180,000, including €60,000 above the original budget. Supporting records for each line still need to be reconciled.

Suppose one recorded transformation produces two candidate headings from that passage:

| Candidate output | Transformation traceability | Support review | Missing premise or limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. “The schedule reports €180,000 in spending attributed to the investment, €60,000 above the original budget; line records remain unreconciled.” | Pass, if the record below resolves to the exact S03 version and scope | Proposed **supported**: it preserves the amount, attribution wording and reconciliation limit | No stronger conclusion added |
| B. “The third founder caused a €60,000 loss.” | The same provenance record can pass | Proposed **not established / unresolved**: S03 does not state individual causation or economic loss | Which transactions caused a loss, what loss occurred, and who caused it |

A minimal inspectable record for either output is:

### Migration check

If a migration moves the S03 citation from candidate A to candidate B, preserve both pairings:

| Stage | Claim–citation pairing | Migration result | Support result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Before | Candidate A → S03 | Recorded | Proposed supported |
| After | Candidate B → S03 | Citation resolves and may be correctly placed syntactically | Not established: the final claim adds loss and individual causation |

This before/after record shows where the unsupported pairing appeared. It does not convert placement, formatting or reference resolution into a semantic verdict.

The source, transformation record, candidate outputs and proposed judgments are fictional teaching material. No agent transformation or migration checker was executed, and no external reader has tested this exercise.

### Reproduce or challenge this review

1. Confirm the `source_ref` resolves to the exact S03 version and scope.
2. Compare every material clause in the chosen output with the source passage.
3. Name the first unsupported clause and the premise it would require.
4. For a migrated citation, compare the before and after pairings and state whether the problem was introduced by migration or already present.
