# Cofounders: review one claim

Three people build a business together. After their relationship breaks down, one faces a €480,000 claim. Shared decisions, a budget overrun, a late warning, lost customers and an incomplete handover must be examined separately.

## Five claims to challenge

Every claim below is **deliberately too strong**. Correct it using the source passages; do not assume the allegation, the rebuttal or our proposed review is true.

| Question | Deliberately unsupported claim | Sources to examine | Preserve in your review |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 — Shared decision and personal acts | “Because all three founders approved the investment, none of the losses can be attributed to the third founder.” | [S02](https://openproof.net/docs/case-brief.html#source-S02), [S03](https://openproof.net/docs/case-brief.html#source-S03), [S04](https://openproof.net/docs/case-brief.html#source-S04) | Approval capped at €120,000, reported spending of €180,000, acknowledged late warning |
| 2 — Customer departure and causation | “The customer message proves that the founder caused the entire €200,000 loss.” | [S01](https://openproof.net/docs/case-brief.html#source-S01), [S05](https://openproof.net/docs/case-brief.html#source-S05), [S08](https://openproof.net/docs/case-brief.html#source-S08) | Multiple reasons given by one customer; a claimed revenue figure is not an established loss |
| 3 — Authority and remaining duties | “After 5 May, the third founder had no operational responsibility.” | [S01](https://openproof.net/docs/case-brief.html#source-S01), [S06](https://openproof.net/docs/case-brief.html#source-S06) | Approval restrictions coexist with retained operational coordination |
| 4 — Handover and missing work | “Handing over access proves that the transfer was complete.” | [S07](https://openproof.net/docs/case-brief.html#source-S07) | Two incomplete customer files and receipt still to confirm |
| 5 — Claim, calculation and attribution | “The €480,000 total is an established debt owed by the third founder.” | [S03](https://openproof.net/docs/case-brief.html#source-S03), [S05](https://openproof.net/docs/case-brief.html#source-S05), [S07](https://openproof.net/docs/case-brief.html#source-S07), [S08](https://openproof.net/docs/case-brief.html#source-S08) | Identity of claimant and beneficiary, calculation, causation and possible double counting remain to examine |

The first task covers question 1. You can examine another question in the same thread by naming its number; avoid trying to answer all five in one first contribution.

## Respond in four short lines

1. **Supported wording:** what can the excerpts actually support?
2. **Sources:** which source IDs and passages support that wording?
3. **Adverse or missing element:** what must remain visible, including what challenges the founder's account?
4. **Next review question:** what should a human examine next?

After the interactive exercise prepares your response, [post it in #44](https://github.com/openproof-net/openproof-rpo/issues/44) if you want public review. Use only the fictional material. For a larger deliverable, dataset or code change, agree scope and applicable rights first.

## Keep the wider context open

The case also asks about business economics, actual authority, the prior situation, resources available to act and escalation into conflict. A chronology alone does not prove causation. An earlier difficulty does not rule out later aggravation; a restriction on authority does not erase every remaining duty.

A document's presence, its reference ID, what its text supports and a professional conclusion are different checks. The [review guide](REVIEW_GUIDE.md) explains how we examine an answer without treating a matching citation as proof of truth.
